# Open tasks — shared rules (AI, ImpactHer, Defence, Smart City, Sport & Healthcare)

All five documented open tasks are sponsored by PROIDEA Sp. z o.o. (Kraków). Each has **8,000 PLN** (gross) and matching rules, judging and AI policy. Their themes differ; see the track files, including [Sport & Healthcare](open-sport-healthcare.md), whose supplied description and rules were read in full.

## Judging criteria (with official definitions)

| Criterion | Weight | What the judges look for |
|---|---|---|
| **Idea & Innovation** | 30% | Uniqueness and creativity of the concept. Groundbreaking, inventive ideas that stand out |
| **Relation to Category** | 20% | How well the project fits the task's theme. It must clearly sit within the scope and meet its objectives |
| **Practical Applicability / Usability** | 20% | Real-world usefulness and ease of use. Practical, functional, addresses real needs, seamless UX |
| **Design** | 20% | **Visual appeal and aesthetics**: UI, graphics and other visual elements |
| **Completeness & Implementation Value** | 10% | Technical robustness, how fully developed it is, and its potential for real-world implementation |

## Submission

**Must include:** project title, team name, team members (1–6), project description, and a **PDF presentation of 10 slides or fewer**.

**May include:** snapshots, code repository, demo links, graphics, other materials.

Polish or English (English recommended; the upload form asks for English).

## Using existing resources

Existing repositories, materials and other resources are allowed **if properly cited**.

## Use of AI policy

- AI tools are permitted at every stage: concept, research, coding, debugging, design and docs.
- The team remains fully responsible for the originality, functionality, security, licensing and legal compliance of the whole solution.
- **Disclose** significant use of AI tools and of external models, APIs, datasets and libraries.
  - No need to share prompt history or the percentage of AI-generated code.
  - Projects are not judged on lines of code written by hand.
- The jury judges the **actual technical work done at HackYeah**: complexity, architecture, integrations, implementation approach, functionality, and **how well the team understands its own system**.
- The team must be able to **explain and defend technical decisions**, including AI-generated parts. Features the team can't explain may lower the technical score.
- Clearly distinguish hackathon work from anything pre-existing. Presenting earlier work as new is not allowed.
- Open-source libraries, frameworks, APIs and models are fine under their licences.
- **Grounds for disqualification:** plagiarism, unauthorized use of third-party IP, misleading the jury about the scope of the work, or hiding a significant pre-existing component.
- Partner tasks may add their own AI rules, which then take precedence.

## Competition terms (the same for every open task)

- Team of 1–6. Work starts "no earlier than 11:00 PM on October 3rd" (probably a typo for AM) and must be submitted by **11:00 PM on October 4th**.
- Submissions go to the HackTribe platform (the descriptions say Challenge Rocket) in English or Polish.
- People related to jury members or sponsor employees are excluded.
- Judging has two phases:
  1. A commission of at least 3 mentors scores the submissions on the platform.
  2. Finalists pitch live to a jury. The two groups may overlap.
- A project needs **≥50% of the points in phase 1** to win a prize. Decisions are final.
- Changes made after the deadline are ignored.
- Copyright is **not** transferred to the sponsor.
- Prizes are paid within 90 days. The jury list is posted on Discord by 4 Oct.
