General research boundaries:
- Climbing completion outcomes are affected by difficulty, practice exposure and route selection; an outcome comparison alone cannot isolate a physiological or technical deficit.
- Instrumented strength/endurance protocols, mobility-distance tests, walking pose validation and calibrated shoulder-angle studies concern particular tasks/equipment/populations; validity does not transfer automatically to a different camera procedure.
- Preview and practice investigations are useful context, but associations, small samples, bundled interventions and uncertain between-group results limit causal recommendations.
- Dynamic ultrasound/MRI investigations of injured fingers cannot validate diagnosis or internal healing estimates from ordinary photographs.
- Engineered ligament studies, biomarker trials and a single patellar-tendon case do not establish a universal climbing dose, recovery schedule, supplement prescription or finger rehabilitation protocol.
- Published research findings should be scoped to the actual study; reader access and abstract-only versus full-text review should be described honestly.
